## The three readings

1. **Is the return concentrated in one year?** — `SOME_YEARS_CARRY_MORE_THAN_OTHERS`: the best year (2025) is worth 26.0% of the CAGR, so removing its bars leaves **19.73%**/yr instead of 26.67%.
2. **Did any year lose money?** — `NO_NEGATIVE_YEAR_IN_SAMPLE`: every calendar year of the sample compounded positive. The instructed rung's worst year is **2023 10.65%**; the in-band rung's is 2023 7.53%.
3. **Is the in-band rung's worst year better per unit of risk?** — `BAND_WORST_YEAR_BETTER_PER_RISK`: return/vol 0.905 vs 0.758 (+0.148). Different-sized books, so the raw pp of #2 is not a risk comparison.

This table is the one that stops a 2025-shaped year from being read as one thing: of the year's 42.98 pp summed, the equity sleeve supplied 15.90 pp and the two legs 27.07 pp -- the sleeve is the piece every rung shares, the lot count is what varies.

## Same rung, two annualised numbers

`results/idx_fut_carry_ladder_risk.json` publishes 1/1 as **22.34%**/yr for rung 1/1; this face rebuilds the same rung as **26.67%**/yr (gap 4.33 pp). The published face weights the equity sleeve by `k` twice; the legs are identical in both, so the gap is the sleeve's k^2-k. Recorded here, not repaired there: that face is a shared face.

## What this face does NOT do

* it does not re-derive a leg or the sleeve: both are imported from the same authority modules the risk face uses;
* it does not rewrite `idx_fut_carry_ladder_risk` (shared face, contention window);
* every reading is in-sample over 1052 bars; a year's return is that year's own compound, never annualised from a partial year.
