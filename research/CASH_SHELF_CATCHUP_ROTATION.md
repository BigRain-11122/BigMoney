# The cash shelf's depressed side: NO_CATCH_UP_IN_THE_SHELF

Primary configuration (H=20, K=5, 8.0 bp/side): the cheap side earns **-40.894 bp** per window with t=-1.74, rank correlation 0.115; the two trips cost **2.016 pp/yr**, so what is left is **-6.937 pp/yr**.

The same ruler on the **rich** side prints -2.921 bp per window (t=-0.16, -2.358 pp/yr after the same two trips).  It is printed so the reader can see the sign of the ordering rather than infer it, and it is **not** a claim: part of it is a level effect (some vehicles accrue faster and stay rich), the t-stat sits under the power line, and separating the two effects is a different question than the one frozen here.

At 2.0 bp/side (the realistic money-ETF spread) the same configuration prints **-1.919 pp/yr**; the gap between the two cost schedules is the whole question, which is why both are printed.

## What this does not claim

- no live order: this is a measurement of a shelf-wide ordering, not a ticket
- survivor bias: membership is r2048's priced census, delisted names never enter
- a 'cheap' money ETF is usually one that paid its accrual early -- the comparison that matters is shelf-equal-weight, which is the baseline here
- costs are a frozen schedule, not a measured spread for any single name
- the richest side is printed beside the cheapest but is NOT a claim: it mixes a level effect (some vehicles simply accrue faster) with a timing effect, and its t-stat on the primary configuration sits under the power line

## Caveat

Catch-up inside a money shelf is a timing transfer between names that all accrue at roughly the same rate.  A positive spread here is not alpha over cash unless it survives the two trips; that is exactly what the net line is.
