# DIGEST 2026-10-30 r1290 bm-b — E262: another window's wide end is not a bound

**Face**: `results/state_tilt/BANDTRANSFER-2026-10-29.json` (+ `bandtransfer_latest.json`)
**Runner**: `scripts/e262_p95_transfer_across_windows.py` (run / verify / selftest 43/43)

**Law**: measurement only. A moved verdict here is a statement about the published numbers, not about the market.

## The question

E259 published a p05..p95 interval per (window, leg, base count) and closed with "the interval is not a
three-seed range". A reader does something with that table that E258/E259 never tested: **borrow a wide end
from another window and judge a row with it.** Two shapes of that act are legitimate questions:

1. is there one window whose p95 is ≥ every other's, so "use the widest" is a safe single number?
2. if a row is judged with a donor's p95, does its verdict move — and in which direction?

## The answer

**No single window is the widest everywhere.** 16 arms (4 measured windows × 2 legs × 2 base counts), the
dominating arm differs by group:

| group | widest window | p95 | spread to the narrowest |
|---|---|---|---|
| self:60 | all_five | 2.1808 | 9.7% |
| self:200 | early_three | 1.7186 | 9.1% |
| cross:60 | all_five | 2.2963 | 16.7% |
| cross:200 | era_2025_now | 1.7816 | 16.8% |

**2 of 48 ordered (donor, recipient) pairs move a verdict**, both `rejections_lost`, both from the widest
arm at `cross:60`:

| donor | recipient | donor p95 | own p95 | delta | row moved |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| all_five | era_2023_2024 | 2.2963 | 2.0161 | +13.9% | star_lag1_residual b=-0.8332 outside→inside |
| all_five | era_2025_now | 2.2963 | 1.9678 | +16.7% | cross b=-0.9113 outside→inside |

Both rows sit just outside a band whose lower end moves under the borrowed multiplier; the wider number
swallows them. Nothing invents a rejection (0 `rejections_invented`): a donor narrower than the row's own
wide end never flips a row in this table, because the era rows' margins on the positive side are large.

## The flat read — what "use the widest" actually costs

One pooled p95 per (leg, base) — the most permissive single number anyone would adopt:

| group | flat p95 | from | rows moved | tight alternative | rows moved at tight |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| cross:60 | 2.2963 | all_five | **2** | 1.9678 | 0 |
| cross:200 | 1.7816 | era_2025_now | 0 | 1.5255 | 0 |
| self:60 | 2.1808 | all_five | 0 | 1.9881 | 0 |
| self:200 | 1.7186 | early_three | 0 | 1.5756 | 0 |

So the pooled maximum is harmless in three of four groups and erases both rejections in the fourth. The
damage is not "pooling is wrong"; it is "pooling is invisible exactly where it matters".

## Cross-base disclosure (never mixed into the matrix)

The multiplier's denominator **is** the base count (`edge(top)/edge(base)`), so a 60-draw multiplier and a
200-draw multiplier of the same leg are not estimates of one quantity: p95 median 2.154 vs 1.597 (self,
ratio 1.35), 2.041 vs 1.635 (cross, ratio 1.25). The face publishes this as `cross_base_offset` and builds no
matrix across base counts.

## Re-judgement disclosure (the r1288 finding, with a number)

E259's row table judged every row on the **self** arm. A residual carries no leg; its coefficient is the cross
coefficient. Resolving each row on the leg its own coefficient identifies moves exactly **1** wide-end verdict:
`era_2023_2024 star_lag1_residual` was interval-dependent (wide: inside) on the self arm and is **outside at
every end** on the cross arm (2.0161 < 2.1748). The face publishes the list in `rejudge_note` and leaves E259's
own face untouched — disclosure, not a correction of the record.

## Pits recorded

1. **`L or {}` / `dict.get` chain on a truthy non-dict** — the first draft's transfer summary summed
   `p.get("n_moved") or 0` over pairs including the one-window blocks that carry no `pairs` key; the block
   guard (`for p in (v.get("pairs") or [])`) is what makes the empty group honest, and a selftest leg pins
   that a single-window group publishes no pair.
2. **A fixture that already sits inside its own band cannot test a widening** — the first "wider donor loses
   a rejection" leg used b=-0.5 against band ±0.3 scaled by 2 (±0.6): the row was inside at its own p95 and
   stayed inside, so the assertion failed for the right reason. Fixtures for a direction need a row that sits
   just outside the *own* band (b=-0.7 vs ±0.6).
3. **An assertion can be wrong about a tie** — `dominator()` returns `None` when two arms tie at the maximum
   (the tie is published in `tied_at_max`); the leg asserting a named winner on a tie was the wrong assertion,
   not the wrong code (r1281 shape).
4. **A `raise` in a fixture is not a measurement** — the ordered-pair count in a two-cell group is 2 (both
   directions), and the donor *for a named recipient* is 1; conflating them made three legs fail on a correct
   implementation.
5. **PowerShell does not take heredocs** (`python - <<'PY'`) — the patch round between the first and second
   selftest run died in the parser (r1288 pit re-confirmed). Patch through a file or the edit tool only.
