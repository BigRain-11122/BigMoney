## The rule

An identity field's *name* does not fix its *question*. Grade the value's reading beside the field's
presence, in three classes that need three different repairs:

| reading | what the stem is saying | repair |
|---|---|---|
| `names_the_producer_that_published_the_face` (10) | correct answer, wrong dialect | normalise the value to the card (add `card`) |
| `names_a_script_the_producer_chose_as_its_subject_not_its_author` (1) | a different question in the same field | rename the field (`subject_study`) **and** add the author card |
| `names_a_script_no_producer_of_this_face_mentions` (0) | a stale or copied value | re-derive it |

Reading 2 is decided mechanically: the value appears as a **module-level string constant** in one of
the face's own producers. That is the producer naming its subject on purpose, and it is what keeps
this study from publishing "CORPUSPIN carries a wrong identity" — a claim that would have been both
false and louder than the finding.

## Pits

- **A field named `study` is not a question.** `study`/`card`/`pin` are in E458's frozen identity
  vocabulary, so presence alone reads as identity; the same field on CORPUSPIN is the pinned
  corpus's name. Any census that counts identity-named fields without reading the value merges an
  author statement with a subject statement (E461's generic-key finding, one layer down).
- **"No producer knows an identity" is the wrong null.** The interesting residue is not "nobody
  knows" (0 here) but "everybody knows and nobody writes it" (11 + 1 here). A census that stops at
  the producer side would have reported the gap as closed.
- **The filename card is the only identity a producer cannot fail to own**, so it is read first and
  published as `card_from_filename`; docstring cards are published as a *set of citations* because a
  docstring cites the studies it builds on (`e368` cites E350/E353/E366, `e369` cites E350/E353/E361).
- **A tie is not a headline.** The verdict needs a strict majority of one class; a plurality is
  published as a split (proved on fixtures, and the earlier "most rows wins" version was replaced
  after the fixture caught it).
