# E224 -- a state gate does not escape the observation wall (r1251, bm-b)

Date: 2026-10-29 · cutoff 2026-10-28 · primary sh510300 (3,503 sessions, 2012-05-28..2026-10-28)
· basket arm 5 ETFs (5,268 sessions, 2005-02-23..)

## The question E223 left open

E223 rejected the frequency ladder and named its own successor: not a faster anchor, but
a rule whose **independent observations** rise without cutting the exposure per
observation -- a continuously-sampled state/volatility gate. This face tests exactly that.

## Frozen before reading

| arm | state | hold |
|---|---|---|
| `vol_low_h10` / `vol_low_h20` | trailing 20-session vol in the bottom tercile of the previous 250 signal values | 10 / 20 sessions |
| `vol_high_h10` / `vol_high_h20` | same, top tercile | 10 / 20 sessions |

Entry at the close of the qualifying session, hold the next H sessions, one round trip
(2 x 13.041bp) per **episode**. While holding the gate is not polled, so episodes never
overlap and no return is counted twice. Excess per episode = held - toll - H x (in-sample
mean daily return), the drift you would have earned unfiltered. First 60% of sessions
in-sample, 8 tests -> bar 2.73.

## The load-bearing reading

The gate is sampled every session, but it cannot produce a continuous stream of
observations: **the hold length caps the episode count at 252/H**, and the gate only
fills part of that cap. At H=10 the ceiling is 25.2/yr and the gate reached 10.5
(41.7% occupancy) -- *below* the 12/yr that the calendar anchor gets for free.

Resolution therefore moves for a different reason than the one that motivated the test:

    calendar (E223)  12.0 obs/yr   MDE 1860.6 bp/yr
    gate best        5.9 obs/yr    MDE  810.7 bp/yr   -> 2.30x tighter

The gate's bar is 2.3x tighter *with half the observations*. What bought it is
`sd_per_episode` (444bp vs 463bp monthly) applied to a longer hold, not the count. The
mechanism is the same one E223 isolated: `MDE_ann = z * sd_per_episode *
sqrt(episodes_per_year / years)`; only the dispersion term moved.

Second, the overlapping-window intuition is wrong in the direction that matters. Treating
every held day as an independent observation -- what a naive pooled t-test silently does
-- yields t values *smaller* than the episode-level ones here (inflation 0.31-0.88), not
larger. Days inside a multi-session hold are less dispersed than independent draws would
be, so the naive reading understates rather than manufactures significance. The reason to
count episodes is that the days are not independent, not that they inflate.

Third: every arm is negative out of sample on the index, including the two "calm regime"
arms whose premise (low vol is a better place to be long) is the most intuitive. The toll
is 26.08bp per episode and the gate pays it 6-10 times a year while holding 40-47% of the
time -- 0.9-1.9 rounds trips worth of cost per year of exposure.

## Carry-forward

- The observation wall is set by the hold, not by the sampling rate. Any rule claiming
  "more observations" must publish episodes/yr beside the ceiling 252/H it is drawing on.
- The only lever measured here that moved the bar was per-episode dispersion, and it moved
  it 2.3x -- still leaving an 810bp/yr threshold, three orders above anything this panel
  has produced.
- Next candidate must reduce dispersion *without* paying a per-episode toll at high
  frequency, i.e. a continuously-held tilt whose weight varies with the state rather than
  a binary in/out gate that pays a round trip each time it turns on.
